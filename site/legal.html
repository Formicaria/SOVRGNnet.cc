Privacy notice — this website

This applies to sovrgnnet.cc, the static site you're reading. It does not apply to any SOVRGNnet instance, including your own.

What we collect

Nothing, deliberately. There are no analytics, no tracking pixels, no advertising, no third-party embeds, and no fonts or scripts loaded from anyone else's servers. This site sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser.

There's no account to create here and no form to fill in, so there is nothing for us to store about you.

What our host sees

The site is served by Cloudflare Pages. Like any web host, Cloudflare processes technical request data — IP address, user agent, requested page — to deliver the page and defend against abuse. We don't attach identities to it or use it to build a profile of you. This is handled under Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Links elsewhere

We link to GitHub and other sites. Once you follow a link, you're under that site's policy, not ours.

Your rights

Rights over personal data — access, correction, erasure, portability, objection — apply to data a controller actually holds. We hold none from this website, so there's nothing to disclose or delete. If you believe otherwise, write to privacy@formicaria.us and we'll answer.

If you run an instance

Once you install SOVRGNnet, you are the operator. Your instance runs on your hardware, under your control. Formicaria has no access to it, receives no telemetry from it, and cannot see, moderate, retrieve, or delete anything on it. We could not comply with a request about your instance's data even if we wanted to, because we don't have it.

That also means the responsibilities are genuinely yours:

  • You are the data controller for everyone on your instance. If you're in the UK or EU and running it for anyone beyond your own household, data protection law likely applies to you.
  • Tell your users the truth about encryption. Messages are stored as plaintext on your server and you can read them. People deserve to know that before they type. The details are here.
  • You're responsible for what's shared on your instance, and for your own backups and security.
  • Local law applies to you, not to us.
Running one for other people? Write down what you collect, how long you keep it, who can see it, and how someone gets their data removed — then tell your users where to find it. It takes twenty minutes and it's the difference between sovereignty and just moving the problem.
